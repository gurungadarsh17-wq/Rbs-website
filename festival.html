Festivals of Nepal

More than 50 festivals a year — Nepal's calendar is a year-long celebration of devotion, community, and joy.

Celebration उत्सव

A Nation that
Celebrates Every Season

Nepal follows the ancient lunar Bikram Sambat calendar, meaning festivals shift each year. But they never stop — every month brings processions, feasts, lights, and music to the streets.

When to Go उत्सवको समय

Festival Calendar

Mar – Apr

होली

Holi — Festival of Colors

The arrival of spring explodes in clouds of coloured powder and water across Nepal. Celebrated wildly in Kathmandu's Basantapur square, Holi marks the victory of good over evil and the end of winter.

Apr – May

बिस्केट जात्रा

Bisket Jatra — Nepali New Year

Bhaktapur's most spectacular festival — two massive wooden chariots carrying the gods Bhairav and Bhadrakali are pulled through the city's narrow medieval streets in a tug of war between east and west.

Jul – Aug

इन्द्र जात्रा

Indra Jatra

Kathmandu's eight-day street festival, honouring Indra, the god of rain. The Living Goddess Kumari is paraded through the old city on a golden chariot, while masked Mahakali and Kumari dances fill the nights.

Sep – Oct

दशैं

Dashain — The Greatest Festival

Nepal's most important festival spans 15 days. Families gather from across the country; goats are sacrificed at Durga temples; elders place tika (a red paste mixed with yoghurt and rice) on the foreheads of the young as a blessing. The skies fill with kites.

Oct – Nov

तिहार

Tihar — Festival of Lights

Nepal's answer to Diwali — five days of oil lamps, flower garlands, and reverence for crows, dogs, cows, oxen, and siblings. On the final day, sisters place tika on their brothers' foreheads in one of Hinduism's most tender rituals.

Nov – Dec

छठ पर्व

Chhath Puja

Worshippers — particularly in the Terai — stand waist-deep in rivers at sunrise and sunset to offer prayers to the sun god Surya. The riverbanks become a vast living tapestry of orange, yellow, and devotion.

Dec – Jan

योमरी पुन्हि

Yomari Punhi

A Newari harvest festival marking the end of the rice harvest — households make yomari (sweet rice-flour dumplings) and exchange them with neighbours. A warm, intimate festival that smells of ghee and new rice.

The Greatest दशैं

Dashain —
The Heart of Nepal

If you can only witness one Nepali festival, make it Dashain. For fifteen days, the entire nation comes alive with the sound of drums, the smell of marigolds, and the sight of generations reuniting under one roof.

15 Days of celebration
तिका Sacred blessing from elders
जमरा Sacred barley sprouts — a symbol of victory
दशैं — Festival of Victory
Festival of Lights

तिहार

Tihar

For five nights, Nepal glows. Every home outlines its windows and doors with tiny oil lamps (diyo), marigold garlands drape doorways, and the streets vibrate with the sound of young women singing deusi-bhailo — a tradition of door-to-door carolling in exchange for blessings and coins.

Tihar is also the festival that celebrates the bond between brothers and sisters. On the fifth day — Bhai Tika — sisters place an elaborate seven-coloured tika on their brothers' foreheads, promising lifelong protection in exchange for the same.

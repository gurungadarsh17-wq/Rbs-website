Culture of Nepal

125 ethnicities. 123 languages. One extraordinary mosaic of humanity.

संस्कृतिको परिचय

A Living Tapestry

Nepal's culture is one of the world's most complex and fascinating — a living crossroads where Indo-Aryan, Tibetan, and indigenous traditions have interwoven for more than two millennia. With 125 officially recognised ethnic groups and 123 languages still spoken today, this small Himalayan nation is one of the most culturally diverse places on earth.

The valley of Kathmandu alone holds 3 UNESCO-listed ancient cities: Kathmandu, Patan (Lalitpur), and Bhaktapur — each a distinct urban expression of Newari civilisation, preserved in wood, stone, and bronze.

The rhythm of daily life here follows an ancient lunar calendar, punctuated by devotion, harvest, and community. Culture here is not a relic in a museum — it walks the streets, rings temple bells, and fills the air with incense.

Each ethnic group carries distinct dress, dialect, customs, and spiritual practices — a nation of nations.

Kathmandu Valley नेवार

The Newari People

Masters of urban civilisation — the Newars built the valley's temples, perfected metalwork and woodcarving, and developed a unique calendar of festivals that still defines valley life today.

High Himalayas शेर्पा

The Sherpa People

Renowned as the world's greatest mountaineers, the Sherpa people of the Solukhumbu region carry a deep Buddhist culture shaped by living at altitudes above 3,500m.

Terai Lowlands थारू

The Tharu People

Indigenous to the tropical Terai, the Tharu have inhabited jungle regions for centuries, developing immunity to malaria and preserving stunning stick-dance traditions.

Artisanship कला र शिल्प

Art & Craftsmanship

थाङ्का चित्रकला

Thangka Painting

Thangka — sacred scroll paintings depicting Buddhist deities, mandalas, and cosmological maps — are among Nepal's most treasured art forms. Painted with mineral pigments and gold on cotton or silk, a single Thangka can take a master artist months to complete.

The tradition is most alive in Bhaktapur and Patan, where families have passed down the exacting craft for generations, with each pigment, each deity's proportion, and each mudra (hand gesture) precisely prescribed by ancient texts.

Traditional Textile
ढाका कपडा

Dhaka Fabric

The geometric diamond and chevron patterns of Dhaka fabric — handwoven on backstrap looms in Palpa and the hills — are the visual signature of Nepali traditional dress. Each colour and pattern carries meaning rooted in the weaver's culture and season.

Spirituality धर्म

Religion &
Spiritual Life

Nepal is the world's only former Hindu kingdom — yet Buddhism and Hinduism here are so deeply intertwined that many Nepalis practise both simultaneously.

हिन्दू धर्म

Hinduism

Over 81% of Nepalis practise Hinduism, with Shiva as the presiding deity. Pashupatinath Temple in Kathmandu is one of Hinduism's holiest sites.

बौद्ध धर्म

Buddhism

The birthplace of Siddhartha Gautama — the Buddha — Nepal holds some of Buddhism's most sacred places, including Lumbini and Boudhanath Stupa.

प्रकृति पूजा

Animism & Shamanism

Many indigenous communities retain animist and shamanistic traditions. The Dhami-Jhankri (shaman healer) remains central to rural spiritual life.

कुमारी

The Living Goddess

Unique to Nepal: the Kumari is a pre-pubescent girl selected as the living embodiment of the goddess Taleju, worshipped by both Hindus and Buddhists.
